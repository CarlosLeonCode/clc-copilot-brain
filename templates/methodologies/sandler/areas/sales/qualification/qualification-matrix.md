# Sandler Qualification Matrix: Pain, Budget and Decision (`sales/qualification/`)

> **Principle:** we qualify before we present. We never give a presentation or send prices or proposals until we have qualified, in this order, the **pain**, the **budget** and the **decision**, inside an **upfront contract**. We look for a fast yes or no: a timely no is worth more than an "I'll let you know" that drags on for weeks.

---

## 1. The qualifiers

| Qualifier | What we confirm | Opinion questions | Green flags | Red flags |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Upfront contract** | Both sides agreed on how long the meeting lasts, what it is for, that either side can say no, and what happens next if it makes sense | • "We have 20 minutes. I'll ask you some questions about how you work today; if it makes sense, we book a next step, and if not, you tell me no with total freedom. Fair?" | • The prospect agrees to the time and purpose.<br>• They accept that a no is a fine answer. | • "Just tell me the price."<br>• No time agreed, or they keep the meeting open-ended. |
| **Pain** | A concrete problem, with an example, that costs the business money or time every month, and that they want to fix now | • "How comfortable are you with what happens today when [problem]?"<br>• "What bothers you most about it?"<br>• "What does it cost you each month?"<br>• "What happens if this stays the same six more months?" | • Gives a recent example.<br>• Works out a monthly loss themselves (sales, bookings or team hours).<br>• Uses words like "worried", "tired", "frustrated". | • "Everything works fine."<br>• Curious, with no example and no figure.<br>• "We'll look at it someday." |
| **Budget** | That investing makes sense next to the monthly loss | • "If you stop losing that [amount] each month, how much would it make sense to invest each month to fix it?"<br>• "What do you pay today for how you handle it?" | • Gives a monthly figure equal to or above the option that fits them.<br>• Compares with what they already pay and finds it reasonable. | • Expects it to be free.<br>• Asks for special prices or bonuses.<br>• Will not talk about money until "seeing the demo". |
| **Decision** | Who decides, how they decide, by when, and that the person who decides will be at the presentation | • "Besides you, who takes part in a decision like this?"<br>• "How do you usually decide something like this, and when would you like it working?"<br>• "If it were up to you, would you go ahead?" (their own view) | • The person who decides is on the call or confirms they will be at the presentation.<br>• There is a date to decide. | • "I'll pass the information to my boss."<br>• "Just send me the demo."<br>• No date and no process. |

---

## 2. Disqualify-fast signals

If any of these conditions is met and not solved by the next agreed follow-up (with a day and time), close the file and log the reason:

1. **No real pain**: after the pain funnel and a negative reverse, the prospect gives no example and no loss figure.
2. **No access to the person who decides**: you asked twice for the meeting with the person who decides, and your contact's own view does not move either.
3. **Budget disconnect**: they expect a free tool or a price below the option that fits them, even next to their own loss figure.
4. **Asks for promotions**: they make the purchase depend on special prices or bonuses we do not offer.
5. **Happy with what they have**: price and simplicity change nothing for them and there is no pain with their current way of working.

---

## 3. Scripts to close the file

Close with respect and as an equal. A clear no is a good result.

### Script A: no access to the person who decides
> "[Name], in the businesses we work with this only works when the person who decides sees it working. Since we could not get time with [name of the person who decides], I would rather close this for now. If they ever want to see it, tell me a day and a time and we pick it up again."

### Script B: no real pain
> "From what you tell me, today things are handled well and the team is not overwhelmed. So it does not make sense for us to keep going. I'm glad we talked it through clearly."

### Script C: budget disconnect
> "You told me this costs you about [amount] a month, and the option that fits you costs [price] a month. If investing still does not make sense, I understand, and I would rather tell you now than waste your time."

---

## 4. Pipeline health review

- **Weekly review**: in the weekly call review, check every deal in stages 2 to 4 against this matrix. A deal without pain, budget and decision does not count as likely.
- **Internal team targets** (not market data): set how many first calls should end in a meeting and how many presentations should close, and review them in the monthly retrospective. When fewer deals close, the problem is usually in how pain, budget or decision were qualified, not in the close.
